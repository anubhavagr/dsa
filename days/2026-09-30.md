## 🔥 Pattern of the Day: Retire the smaller side
Both water problems put a pointer at each end and ask how much the pair contributes — an area today, trapped water above a bar.
The cap is always set by the weaker side, so moving the stronger side inward can only shrink the span without ever lifting the cap: that is the exchange argument that makes the greedy safe.
Reach for it when a pair-of-ends quantity depends on min(left side, right side).
Invariant (trap): the pointer you are standing on already knows its final water level, because the opposite side is guaranteed at least as tall as its own running max.
Complexity to narrate: one O(n) pass, O(1) extra space — strictly better than prefix-max arrays' O(n) space, and that upgrade is the standard follow-up question, so volunteer it unprompted.
Contrast the two objectives in one breath: container wants the best single pair, trap wants a sum over every bar — the retirement rule survives the change of question.
The prefix-max formulation (left[i] and right[i] maxima, then sum of min(left, right) − height[i]) is your fallback and your whiteboard explanation — draw it, then optimize it away.
Both problems reward a hand example before code: draw four bars, fill the water with a pencil, and only then start typing.

```python
def max_area(height):                      # container: area between two walls
    l, r, best = 0, len(height) - 1, 0
    # area = width × the shorter wall — the shorter wall is the bottleneck
    while l < r:
        span = r - l
        best = max(best, span * min(height[l], height[r]))
        if height[l] < height[r]:
            l += 1                          # moving the TALLER side can never
        else:                               # help: span shrinks and the area
            r -= 1                          # stays capped by the shorter wall
    return best
```

```python
def trap(height):                           # water above each bar
    l, r = 0, len(height) - 1
    l_max = r_max = 0
    water = 0
    # each bar's water is decided at the moment that bar is retired
    while l < r:
        if height[l] < height[r]:
            # the right wall is taller, so water at l is limited ONLY by the
            # best wall on its own left — l_max is already final for bar l
            l_max = max(l_max, height[l])
            water += l_max - height[l]
            l += 1
        else:
            r_max = max(r_max, height[r])   # symmetric, mirrored
            water += r_max - height[r]
            r -= 1
    return water
```

Watch-outs:
- Two prefix-max arrays also solve trap in O(n) time and O(n) space — mention that first, then win the follow-up with the O(1)-space version above.
- Ask which side to process on ties (height[l] == height[r]) — both branches stay correct there; noticing the tie is what matters in the room.
- If the interviewer asks for one pass and O(1) space, point at the two running maxima — you are already standing in the answer.
- Container maximizes an area; trap sums water above bars — different objective, same retirement rule. Say which one you are solving.
- Decide whether water is added for the bar you are leaving before or after the move, then verify on `[2,0,2]` and `[4,2,3]` until it is muscle memory.
