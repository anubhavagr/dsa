## 📖 Phase 4 — Reference checkpoints (open only AFTER your attempt)
What a strong L5 answer includes:
- Write path = WAL append + memtable insert, O(1) amortized — then flush to immutable SSTables
- Leveled compaction BOUNDS read amplification at the cost of write amplification — the tradeoff is stated, not just the mechanism
  - Say it: "Leveled compaction trades write amplification for bounded read amplification — I'm paying writes to protect the tail."
- Bloom filters per SSTable skip most misses cheaply — and you know they only answer "definitely not here"
  - Say it: "A bloom filter only says 'definitely not here' — a false positive still costs a disk read, just rarely."
- Read path = memtable → newest-to-oldest SSTables, each gated by its bloom filter
  - Say it: "Memtable first, then newest-to-oldest SSTables, each gated by its bloom."
- Consistent hashing + virtual nodes → even load distribution + small migration units
- Replication factor 3, quorum reads/writes with R + W > N (e.g. R=2, W=2) — and what each config trades
  - Say it: "R plus W greater than N — two and two of three — overlap guarantees one fresh copy."
- Rebalancing = vnode ownership transfer with dual-write / read-both during migration — no cliff, just a hump
- TTL = lazy expiration on read + dropped at compaction — a background sweep is the wrong answer, say why
  - Say it: "Expiry is lazy — checked on read, dropped at compaction — a background sweep would spike the very p99 I promised."
- Hot key options ranked: client-side cache > replicate-on-read > key duplication (breaks consistency) — ranked out loud
  - Say it: "Client cache first, replicate-on-read second, key duplication last — it breaks consistency, so it's the option I defend, not choose."
- p99 discipline: GC-tuned (or GC-free) runtime, slab allocation, thread-per-core — tail latency is a systems discipline
